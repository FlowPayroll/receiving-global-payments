# 🇳🇬 Receiving international client payments in Nigeria

International client work is a significant part of Nigeria’s freelance economy. Payment decisions often depend on access to foreign-currency banking, conversion to naira, and the total cost of receiving funds.

## Payment options to compare

- **USDT and stablecoins:** Digital-asset transfers may be considered when appropriate, though conversion access, legal requirements, custody, and counterparty risks matter.
- **Payoneer:** Used in some international contractor and marketplace workflows; availability depends on the account and product.
- **SWIFT and domiciliary bank accounts:** Traditional bank routes may be possible for eligible accounts, with fees, documentation, and processing requirements.
- **Exchange or P2P conversion:** Potential conversion routes for eligible users, but scams, settlement disputes, and rules require care.

## Practical guidance

- Compare the final NGN amount after all conversion and cash-out costs.
- Where exchanges or P2P platforms are permitted, verify counterparties and use appropriate safeguards; do not release assets based on screenshots or unconfirmed payments.
- Keep invoices and payment evidence, and check current Nigerian banking, digital-asset, and tax rules.

## Using USDT carefully

USDT is designed to reference the US dollar, but its value and redemption are not guaranteed. Blockchain transactions may be irreversible. Before receiving or sending funds, confirm the correct token and supported **TRC20, ERC20, or BEP20** network on both sides; consider a small test transfer and keep transaction hashes, conversion rates, and receipts. Check whether digital-asset activity and local conversion routes are permitted for your circumstances.
